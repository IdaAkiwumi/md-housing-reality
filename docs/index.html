Understanding the numbers

What does median mean?

The middle value. Half fall below it and half above. Not the same as an average.

What does gross rent include?

Rent plus tenant-paid utilities, for the survey period. Not today's asking rents.

What does the 30 percent benchmark mean?

The income that would make median rent equal to 30% of income. A comparison point, not a guarantee a household can afford it.

Why margins of error?

These are survey estimates, not counts. A $1,500 rent with a $50 margin means roughly $1,450–$1,550 at the Census Bureau's 90% confidence level.

What is the current market rent figure?

It comes from the Zillow Observed Rent Index — a monthly smoothed estimate of asking rent across all rentals in the county. It updates every month and reflects today's market, not the 5-year Census window. If it looks much higher than the Census median, that gap is the signal: the Census average lags behind what new renters pay now.

Zillow does not publish a figure for every county. Markets with too few listings — in Maryland, usually Kent and Somerset — have no market rent figure. Their Census median rent is still shown.

What is renter household income?

It comes from ACS table B25119 — the median income of households that rent, not all households. Renter median income is usually much lower than the overall median, which is why renter rent burden is often higher than the all-household ratio.

What can trends tell us?

Changes across 2010–2014, 2015–2019, and 2020–2024, in nominal dollars. Different households each window, no significance testing.

Renter housing costs

Includes households spending 30%+ of income on gross rent, from ACS table B25070. No margin of error; small county differences are not meaningful rankings.

What does the refresh date mean?

When the site retrieved the data. Not a new Census release date. Release years are reviewed before updating.

What could change?

These figures help ask questions about rent stabilization, supply, subsidies, and ownership costs. They do not prove a policy will solve affordability.

Technical calculations and data references

Rent: B25064. All-household income: B19013. Renter income: B25119 (variable 003E). Home value: B25077. Rent burden: B25070 (categories 007–010 over 001 minus 011). Market rent: Zillow ZORI, county-level, smoothed seasonally adjusted.

30% benchmark: monthly rent × 12 ÷ 0.30. Percent change: (later ÷ earlier − 1) × 100.

Why this exists

I'm Ida Akiwumi, a UX designer. After being laid off last summer, I started thinking about how I could use my UX and data skills to build something useful beyond my next job.

Housing affordability affects communities across Maryland, but understanding how much rent burdens people can mean digging through multiple Census tables and overlapping survey periods. I wanted to make that information easier to find, compare, and explore in one place.

So I built Maryland Housing Burden, a free tool comparing renter burden across all 24 Maryland jurisdictions, alongside data that helps put reported rents into context.

The data refreshes automatically every month. No ads, no tracking, no cookies.
